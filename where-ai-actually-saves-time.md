Every services business is being pitched AI right now, and most of the pitches skip the part that actually matters: which parts of the work benefit, and which parts still need a person who understands the client.

## Where it clearly works

Structured, repeatable work is where AI earns its keep fastest, first-pass drafts, triage, summarizing long threads into a decision someone can act on. The time saved there is real and it compounds, because it's time that was previously spent on work nobody enjoyed doing anyway.

## Where it still needs a human in the loop

Anything involving judgment about a specific client relationship, negotiation, or reading a room still needs a person. AI can prepare the ground, but the accountability for the outcome should stay with someone who can be held to it.

## The practical takeaway

Treat AI Employees as leverage for your team, not a replacement for judgment. The businesses getting real value are the ones that redesigned the workflow around what AI is actually good at, instead of bolting AI onto a workflow that was designed for people.
