A 25-person NVOCC headquartered in the UK, with teams in the USA, UAE, India, and Poland, ran every client and vendor conversation over email. Booking requests, shipping line quotes, document approvals, and invoices all lived in inboxes. Nobody could say with confidence which bookings were completed, which were in progress, and which were still waiting on a client or a vendor.

## Why email breaks down for an NVOCC

An NVOCC sits between the shipper and the shipping line, so every booking pulls in clients, carriers, and agents across several countries and time zones. Cut-offs don't move, and a booking that slips through the cracks becomes a missed sailing. Email kept the record of what was said, but it couldn't show where each booking actually stood. Status lived in the head of whoever had last read the thread.

## One pipeline every booking moves through

We chose a help desk and ticketing platform that matched the team's requirements and set it up as the single place where bookings live, with every request tracked as New, Pending, In Progress, or Completed. Active bookings move through the stages the business already worked in: Proposal, Booked, Transit, Documentation, Port & Customs, Loaded to Ship, and the vessel's start date. The pipeline ends at a dashboard for raising invoices, so finished bookings don't sit waiting to be billed.

Client and vendor conversations are tracked against the same booking. A pending item shows whether it's waiting on the client or on a shipping line, instead of being buried somewhere in a thread.

## Pricing without the phone calls

Shipping line rates change constantly, and every booking used to start with a round of calls to check availability and price. We built a custom dashboard that pulls live availability and rates directly from the shipping lines' APIs. The team enters the client's requirements, and the dashboard shows which containers are available, what they cost, and how that compares across lines.

Not every shipping line offers an API. When none of the connected lines can take a booking, the dashboard tells the team which other lines to contact and whether to call or email each one, so the decision takes seconds instead of a guess. Those manual contacts now come up only two or three times a week, where before every booking needed a call.

## Six months of pilot before going live

We ran the new pipeline as a six-month pilot before switching it to production, so the stages and dashboards were shaped by real bookings rather than by a whiteboard plan. It has now been running live for eight months.

## What changed

Before, bookings depended on one person in a five-member booking team who knew where everything stood. Now all five work bookings in parallel, because the pipeline shows each of them what needs doing and where. The team has gone from handling 5–7 bookings a day to 20–30, and it's converting more of them into confirmed business.
