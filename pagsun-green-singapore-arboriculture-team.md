A 15-person arboriculture and horticulture company in Singapore came to us with a familiar problem: every tree assessment lived on paper or in whatever app the assessor personally preferred that week. Two independent arborists who work with them on overflow jobs added a third and fourth format into the mix. Nothing rolled up into one place, and nothing was structured enough to compare across sites or over time.

## What "structured" actually meant here

Arborists don't want more software between them and the tree. The brief was narrow on purpose: one assessment format, fillable fast in the field, that captures the same fields every time — species, condition, risk factors, recommended action — regardless of who's doing the assessment or which client it's for.

## Rolling out across employees and freelancers

The harder part wasn't the software, it was getting four different working styles onto one system without slowing anyone down mid-season. We onboarded the in-house team first, kept the assessment form close to what they already did on paper, then brought the two freelance arborists on afterward once the format had settled. Freelancers needed to move between this client and their own other jobs without friction, so access is scoped per engagement rather than requiring a company-wide account.

## What changed

Assessments that used to live in someone's notebook or a personal spreadsheet now land in one system the moment they're done. Recurring maintenance and flagged hazards are scheduled from the same record instead of being tracked separately, which matters most for the cases that used to fall through the cracks between one assessment and the next site visit.

## What we'd tell another crew considering this

Don't try to digitize everything on day one. We shipped the assessment workflow first because that's where the daily pain was, and left scheduling and reporting to follow once the core habit had stuck. A tool that changes too much at once is a tool crews quietly stop using.
