# AI Employee Case Study Playbook

Oct 7, 2026 · @Sundar Rajan

## How to use this playbook

Every AI Employee case study opens and closes the same way. You only choose the middle: answer one question in the next section, pick Framework A, B or C, and fill in its formulas.

**Fixed on every case study (never changes)**

1. Industry tag (the client's industry, e.g. Legal), title, date, "Client identity protected"
2. **Challenge and Solution table**: 4 to 5 rows
3. **Results block**: 2 to 4 figures, shown as Projected vs. Actual
4. *Middle sections: from your chosen framework*
5. What changed
6. Why we don't name our clients
7. CTA, then Sundar's card

**One rule for the table on AI Employee pages:** at least one Solution cell must state a boundary, meaning something the AI Employee does *not* do. This is the fastest trust signal you have.

**Why Projected vs. Actual:** the AI Employee site promises that projected and production numbers are tracked side by side. Showing that on every case study proves the promise is real.

## Pick your framework

Ask the questions in order and stop at the first "yes".

1. **Was data, confidentiality or compliance the client's main worry before saying yes?** (regulated industry, client records, on-premise deployment, wrote a data policy with us) → **Framework C: Safe Deployment**
2. **Does the AI Employee produce work output that people used to produce by hand?** (drafts, checks, data entry, reports, replies) → **Framework B: Workload Takeover**
3. **Does the AI Employee mainly read documents and answer questions?** (status, lookups, summaries, "where does this stand?") → **Framework A: Answer Engine**

|  | A: Answer Engine | B: Workload Takeover | C: Safe Deployment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The AI Employee... | Reads and answers | Does the routine work | Works inside strict rules |
| Reader's silent question | "Will its answers be right?" | "How much time will I really get back?" | "Is my data safe with this?" |
| Best for | Law, consulting, ops teams with many files | Finance, admin, sales ops, support | Legal, healthcare, finance, enterprise IT |
| Results lead with | Time to answer, accuracy | Hours reclaimed, volume handled | Accuracy bar met, zero data leaving policy |
| Central exhibit | A sample question and sourced answer | Before/after workflow | "Does / Never does" boundary box |
| Example on site | Law firm case tracking | (next case) | (next case) |

**Tie-breaker:** if two fit, pick the one that matches what the *reader* is most afraid of, not what the AI Employee is best at.

## Framework A: Answer Engine

**Choose when** the AI Employee reads files, records or documents and answers questions about them. The reader worries the answers might be wrong. Voice: careful and precise.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *How a \[size\] \[industry\] in \[country\] uses an AI Employee to \[answer what\]*
  - How a 12-person law firm in India uses an AI Employee to track case status
- *\[Question people ask\], answered in seconds for a \[size\] \[industry\]*
  - "Where does this shipment stand?", answered in seconds for a 40-person freight forwarder
- *Giving every \[role\] the full picture of \[what\] at a \[size\] \[industry\]*
  - Giving every partner the full picture of every matter at a 12-person law firm

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *Nobody / only \[one person\] could see \[information\] without \[slow step\].*
- Solution: *An AI Employee that reads \[sources\] and answers \[question\], with every answer linked to its source.*

**Results block metrics:** time to answer a question (before vs. now), answer accuracy rate, questions handled per week, time to get oriented on an unfamiliar file.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *\[Client\] has \[volume of files\]. Answers about \[topic\] depended on \[person or habit\]. We deployed an AI Employee that reads \[sources\] to close that gap.*

**Middle sections**

1. **What it reads and what it answers:** sources, the questions it handles, and a short example question and answer.
2. **Why this mattered at their size:** the cost of not knowing (lost time, slow client replies).
3. **How answers stay verifiable:** source links, the accuracy bar agreed before go-live, and what happens when it's unsure.
4. **Where it stops:** what it never does (no filing, no judgments, no sending).

**Central exhibit:** a sample question and sourced answer, anonymised.

**Closing line formula:** *Status questions that used to mean \[old delay\] are now \[new result\], and the source is always one click away.*

**CTA formula:** *Is there a question your team answers again and again? Tell us the one job and get your free AI Employee Success Rate Score.*

## Framework B: Workload Takeover

**Choose when** the AI Employee does repetitive work people used to do by hand: drafts, checks, data entry, reconciliations, report prep, first replies. The reader wants to know how much time they really get back. Voice: practical and numeric.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *How a \[size\] \[industry\] reclaimed \[X hours\] a month by handing \[task\] to an AI Employee*
  - How a 20-person accounting firm reclaimed 46 hours a month by handing invoice checks to an AI Employee
- *From \[old time\] to \[new time\]: an AI Employee for \[task\] at a \[size\] \[industry\]*
  - From 2 days to 2 hours: an AI Employee for monthly reporting at a 30-person distributor
- *What happened when a \[size\] \[industry\] gave \[task\] to an AI Employee*
  - What happened when a 15-person agency gave first-draft proposals to an AI Employee

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *\[Role\] spent \[time\] every \[period\] on \[task\], instead of \[higher-value work\].*
- Solution: *An AI Employee that \[does task\], with \[named person\] reviewing before \[it goes out / is approved\].*

**Results block metrics:** hours reclaimed per month (projected vs. actual), turnaround time, volume handled per day or week, error or rework rate.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *\[Client\] spent \[time\] every \[period\] on \[task\]. It was necessary, repetitive and took \[role\] away from \[real work\]. We scoped the job, agreed the KPIs, and deployed an AI Employee to take it on.*

**Middle sections**

1. **The job before:** the steps, who did them, how long each took. Short and concrete.
2. **What the AI Employee took on, and what stayed with the team:** the split of work, step by step.
3. **The KPIs we agreed first:** the targets set in the Strategic Implementation Document, before any build.
4. **What the team does with the time:** where the reclaimed hours went.

**Central exhibit:** a before/after workflow, showing which steps moved to the AI Employee and which stayed human.

**Closing line formula:** *\[Task\] now takes \[new time\] instead of \[old time\], and \[role\] spends that time on \[higher-value work\].*

**CTA formula:** *Which task eats your team's week? Tell us the one job and see its readiness score in about 60 seconds, free.*

## Framework C: Safe Deployment

**Choose when** the client's main hesitation was data, confidentiality, compliance or control: a regulated industry, sensitive client records, deployment on their own infrastructure, or a data policy written with us. The reader worries more about risk than about results. Voice: calm, exact, no hype.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *Deploying an AI Employee inside a \[industry\]'s data policy, without changing it*
  - Deploying an AI Employee inside a hospital group's data policy, without changing it
- *An AI Employee on \[client\]'s own infrastructure: how a \[size\] \[industry\] kept every record in-house*
  - An AI Employee on its own servers: how a 50-person wealth manager kept every record in-house
- *How a \[size\] \[industry\] adopted AI with a named person accountable for every action*
  - How a 25-person insurance broker adopted AI with a named person accountable for every action

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *\[Client\] couldn't use AI on \[data\] because \[policy, regulation or risk\].*
- Solution: *An AI Employee deployed within \[policy / on own infrastructure\], with \[human check\] before \[action\].*

**Results block metrics:** accuracy bar agreed vs. achieved, records that left the client's environment (target: zero), human approvals per action, time from consultation to production, plus one productivity number.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *\[Client\] wanted \[benefit\] but couldn't risk \[data concern\]. Before building anything, we reviewed their data policy and agreed where responsibility sits. The AI Employee was then built to fit inside it.*

**Middle sections**

1. **The concern, in their words:** what made them hesitate.
2. **Policy first:** what we reviewed, what we wrote (if they had no policy), and where the data stays.
3. **Human in the loop:** who approves what, and how every action ties back to a named person.
4. **Proof before go-live:** the demo on sample data, and the accuracy bar it had to clear.
5. **Running in production:** the result, plus how it is monitored and supported.

**Central exhibit:** a "Does / Never does" boundary box, two columns listing what the AI Employee handles and what stays with people.

**Closing line formula:** *\[Client\] now uses AI on \[data\] without \[risk\], because the AI Employee was built around their rules, not the other way round.*

**CTA formula:** *Want AI but worried about your data? Start with the free readiness score, then we'll scope it within your policy.*

## Results block rules

Show 2 to 4 figures as **Projected / Actual**, with one small line under them saying how and over what period they were measured.

| Metric | Projected | Actual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours reclaimed / month | 40 hrs | 46 hrs |
| Answer accuracy | 90% | 94% |
| Time to answer a status question | under 1 min | about 20 sec |

*Example layout only. Measured over \[X\] weeks of production use.*

**No hard numbers yet? Use one of these instead, in this order of strength:**

1. **A range with its method:** "About 3 to 4 hours saved each week, from the team's own time log"
2. **A count:** "312 status questions answered in the first month"
3. **A before/after time:** "Answered on the spot, instead of the next day"
4. **A milestone:** "Cleared the 90% accuracy bar in the demo, before go-live"

Never leave the block vague ("saves lots of time") or empty. For the law firm case, add at least a count or a before/after time.
