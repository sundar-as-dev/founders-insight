# Pagsun Green Case Study Playbook

Oct 7, 2026 · @Sundar Rajan

## How to use this playbook

Every Pagsun Green case study opens and closes the same way. You only choose the middle: answer the questions in the next section, pick Framework A, B or C, and fill in its formulas.

**Fixed on every case study (never changes)**

1. Industry tag (the client's industry, e.g. Legal), title, date, "Client identity protected"
2. **Challenge and Solution table**: 4 to 5 rows
3. **Results block**: 2 to 4 figures, shown as Before / Now
4. *Middle sections: from your chosen framework*
5. What changed
6. What we'd tell another crew (one short paragraph of honest advice)
7. Why we don't name our clients
8. CTA, then Sundar's card

**One rule for the table on Green pages:** at least one Solution cell must show what the crew *kept*, such as "form kept close to the paper sheet they already used". Field teams fear disruption more than they want features, so low change is a selling point.

**Voice for all Green pages:** plain words, short sentences, the crew's own vocabulary (site visit, TRAQ, zone, hazard, crew). No software jargon.

**Why Before / Now (not Projected / Actual):** Green is a product crews adopt, not a custom build with agreed KPIs. Readers want to see the field reality before and after.

## Pick your framework

Ask the questions in order and stop at the first "yes".

1. **Is the main win about clients?** (reports clients trust, winning or renewing contracts, defending a risk rating, proof that work was done) → **Framework B: Reports Clients Trust**
2. **Is the main win about running many properties, crews or jobs?** (scheduling, recurring maintenance, hazards followed up, nothing falling through the cracks) → **Framework C: Every Property, Every Crew**
3. **Is the main win about getting everyone assessing the same way?** (paper or mixed apps replaced, freelancers onboarded, one consistent format) → **Framework A: One Way to Assess**

|  | A: One Way to Assess | B: Reports Clients Trust | C: Every Property, Every Crew |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The story is about... | The crew in the field | The client receiving the report | The office running the business |
| Reader | Crew lead, arborist, small firm owner | Firm owner selling to property managers, councils, estates | Owner of a growing multi-crew firm |
| Reader's silent question | "Will my crew actually use it?" | "Will it help me win and keep clients?" | "Can it run my whole operation?" |
| Results lead with | Assessments done in-app, time per assessment | Report turnaround, reports sent, contracts kept | Properties managed, missed follow-ups, overdue hazards |
| Central exhibit | Sample assessment record or form screenshot | Sample signed report (anonymised) | One week of the job calendar |
| Example on site | Singapore arboriculture team | (next case) | (next case) |

**Tie-breaker:** pick the framework that matches who would buy Pagsun Green after reading it. Crew-led buyers get A, sales-minded owners get B, operations-minded owners get C.

## Framework A: One Way to Assess

**Choose when** the win is getting a crew, or a crew plus freelancers, onto one consistent assessment method. The reader is close to the field and worries the crew will reject new software. Voice: plain and practical.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *Standardizing \[assessment type\] for a \[size\] \[trade\] team in \[city\]*
  - Standardizing tree assessments for a 15-person arboriculture team in Singapore
- *From \[number\] formats to one: how a \[size\] \[trade\] crew moved off paper*
  - From four formats to one: how a 15-person arboriculture crew moved off paper
- *Getting \[employees\] and \[freelancers\] assessing the same way at a \[trade\] firm in \[city\]*
  - Getting 13 staff and 2 freelance arborists assessing the same way in Singapore

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *Assessments lived in \[paper / notebooks / personal apps\], so \[consequence\].*
- Solution: *One structured \[assessment\] in Pagsun Green, kept close to \[what they already did\].*

**Results block metrics:** assessment formats in use (before vs. now), share of assessments done in-app, time per assessment, crew members still active after \[X\] months.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *A \[size\] \[trade\] firm in \[city\] ran assessments on \[paper / mixed apps\]. \[Who else\] added \[more formats\]. Nothing could be compared across sites or over time.*

**Middle sections**

1. **A day in the field before:** one short scene: the clipboard, the photos on a phone, the typing-up at night.
2. **What "structured" meant here:** the fields captured every time, and what was deliberately left out.
3. **Rollout order:** who went first, who came later, and why that order.
4. **Still in use:** adoption after \[X\] weeks or months, which is the real proof for field teams.

**Central exhibit:** a sample assessment record or form screenshot, anonymised.

**Closing line formula:** *Assessments that used to live in \[old place\] now land in one system the moment they're done, the same way, whoever does them.*

**CTA formula:** *Running assessments on paper? Talk to us and we'll show you Pagsun Green on your own properties.*

## Framework B: Reports Clients Trust

**Choose when** the win shows up on the client's side: faster or more professional reports, risk ratings the firm can defend, photo proof that work was done, or contracts won or renewed. The reader is an owner who sells to property managers, councils, condos or estates. Voice: confident and specific.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *How a \[size\] \[trade\] firm in \[city\] turned site visits into same-day reports clients trust*
  - How a 10-person tree care firm in Kuala Lumpur turned site visits into same-day reports
- *Defensible risk ratings for \[client type\]: a \[trade\] firm's switch to ISA TRAQ in Pagsun Green*
  - Defensible risk ratings for town councils: an arborist firm's switch to ISA TRAQ in Pagsun Green
- *Proof the work happened: how a \[trade\] firm keeps \[client type\] contracts with photo records*
  - Proof the work happened: how a landscaping firm keeps condo contracts with photo records

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *\[Client type\] got \[slow / inconsistent / unsigned\] reports, so \[risk: disputes, lost renewals, liability\].*
- Solution: *One-click signed reports from each \[assessment / visit\], with \[ratings calculated / dated photos\] behind them.*

**Results block metrics:** report turnaround (days before vs. same day now), reports sent per month, disputes or rework requests, contracts renewed or won since switching.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *A \[size\] \[trade\] firm serves \[client type\] across \[number\] properties. Reports took \[time\] to type up and looked different every time. When a client questioned \[a rating / a job\], there was no clean record to point to.*

**Middle sections**

1. **What the client used to receive:** the old report, its delay and its weak spots.
2. **The standard behind the report:** ISA TRAQ ratings calculated by the system, dated photos, fixed fields.
3. **From site visit to signed PDF:** the steps, and how long they take now.
4. **How clients responded:** a renewal, a won tender, fewer questions. Use an anonymous quote if possible.

**Central exhibit:** a sample signed report page, anonymised.

**Closing line formula:** *Every report now goes out \[same day\], built on the same standard, with the photos to back it up.*

**CTA formula:** *Want reports your clients never question? Talk to us and we'll show you a sample from your own site.*

## Framework C: Every Property, Every Crew

**Choose when** the win is running the business: many properties, several crews, recurring maintenance and flagged hazards that no longer slip between visits. The reader is the owner or office manager of a growing firm. Voice: organised and calm.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *Running \[number\] properties and \[number\] crews from one system at a \[trade\] firm in \[city\]*
  - Running 120 properties and 4 crews from one system at a landscaping firm in Singapore
- *No hazard left behind: how a \[size\] \[trade\] firm tracks every follow-up*
  - No hazard left behind: how a 25-person arborist firm tracks every follow-up
- *How a \[trade\] firm grew from \[X\] to \[Y\] crews without losing track of a single job*
  - How a horticulture firm grew from 2 to 5 crews without losing track of a single job

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *\[Recurring maintenance / flagged hazards / crew schedules\] lived in \[whiteboard / spreadsheet / someone's head\], so \[things slipped\].*
- Solution: *Jobs scheduled straight from the \[assessment / property record\], with each crew seeing only their own list.*

**Results block metrics:** properties and trees or zones managed, missed or overdue follow-ups (before vs. now), jobs scheduled per week, time spent planning the week.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *A \[size\] \[trade\] firm in \[city\] looks after \[number\] properties with \[number\] crews. Schedules lived in \[place\], and follow-ups depended on someone remembering. As the firm grew, \[what started slipping\].*

**Middle sections**

1. **What slipped before:** one real example of a missed follow-up or double-booked crew.
2. **One record per property:** inventory, history and open issues in one place.
3. **From assessment to scheduled job:** how a flagged hazard becomes a dated task for the right crew.
4. **Crew access:** each crew member's own login, scoped to their own work.
5. **Growing without chaos:** what happened when they added properties or crews.

**Central exhibit:** one week of the job calendar, anonymised, showing crews and recurring jobs.

**Closing line formula:** *Every \[hazard / maintenance visit\] now has a date and an owner, so nothing waits on someone remembering.*

**CTA formula:** *Juggling properties and crews on a whiteboard? Talk to us and we'll set up a week of your jobs in Pagsun Green.*

## Results block rules

Show 2 to 4 figures as **Before / Now**, with one small line under them saying over what period they were measured.

| Metric | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assessment formats in use | 4 | 1 |
| Time from site visit to client report | 3 days | Same day |
| Overdue hazard follow-ups | Not tracked | 0 open past due |

*Example layout only. Measured over \[X\] months of live use.*

**No hard numbers yet? Use one of these instead, in this order of strength:**

1. **A count:** "1,240 trees assessed in-app in the first quarter"
2. **A before/after time:** "Reports sent the same day, instead of typed up at the end of the week"
3. **An adoption fact:** "All 15 crew members and both freelancers still using it after 6 months"
4. **A formats fact:** "Four assessment formats down to one"

"Not tracked" is a valid Before value: it shows the problem honestly. For the Singapore case, add at least a count and an adoption fact.
