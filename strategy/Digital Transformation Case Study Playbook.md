# Digital Transformation Case Study Playbook

Oct 7, 2026 · @Sundar Rajan

## How to use this playbook

Every Digital Transformation case study opens and closes the same way. You only choose the middle: answer the questions in the next section, pick Framework A, B or C, and fill in its formulas.

**Fixed on every case study (never changes)**

1. Industry tag (the client's industry, e.g. Legal), title, date, "Client identity protected"
2. **Challenge and Solution table**: 4 to 5 rows
3. **Results block**: 2 to 4 figures, shown as Before / Now
4. *Middle sections: from your chosen framework*
5. How we rolled it out (pilot, phases, how long it has run live)
6. What changed
7. Why we don't name our clients
8. CTA, then Sundar's card

**One rule for the table on DT pages:** at least one row must show how disruption was avoided, such as a pilot, a phased rollout, or old and new systems running side by side. Buyers of transformation fear the business stopping mid-project more than they fear the cost.

**Voice for all DT pages:** operational and numeric. Lead with the business outcome, then explain the technology in plain words.

**Why a rollout section is fixed here:** the NVOCC page's six-month pilot is one of its strongest trust signals. Every DT case should show the work was proven on real operations before it went live.

## Pick your framework

Ask the questions in order and stop at the first "yes".

1. **Was the main deliverable a plan, a roadmap or insight from data, rather than a running system?** (strategy, prioritisation, dashboards and analytics for decisions) → **Framework C: Roadmap and Insight**
2. **Did we replace or build core technology?** (legacy system migrated, move to cloud, new app, portal, website or commerce channel) → **Framework B: Built to Scale**
3. **Did we change how daily work flows through the team?** (email, spreadsheets or paper replaced with a tracked pipeline, manual steps automated) → **Framework A: Process Overhaul**

|  | A: Process Overhaul | B: Built to Scale | C: Roadmap and Insight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The story is about... | How work moves | What the business now runs on | Where the business should go next |
| Reader | COO, operations head, founder running ops | Founder, CTO, head of digital | CEO, MD, board-level decision maker |
| Reader's silent question | "Will my team handle more without hiring?" | "Will it launch on time and hold up as we grow?" | "Where do I spend first, and will it pay back?" |
| Pagsun services it covers | Process & Automation, Employee Experience | Technology & Cloud, Digital Products & Channels, Customer Experience | Strategy, Data & AI |
| Results lead with | Throughput, turnaround, people working in parallel | Uptime, speed, users or orders through the new channel, systems retired | Opportunities ranked, payback, time to a decision |
| Central exhibit | Lifecycle diagram of the new pipeline | Before/after architecture sketch | The priority roadmap or a dashboard view |
| Example on site | NVOCC booking pipeline | (next case) | (next case) |

**Tie-breaker:** if a project spans two (for example, a roadmap that led to a new pipeline), pick the framework for the part with the strongest numbers, and mention the other in one line.

## Framework A: Process Overhaul

**Choose when** the win is how work moves through the team: email threads, spreadsheets or paper replaced by one tracked pipeline, and manual steps automated. The reader runs operations and wants more output without more headcount. Voice: operational and numeric.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *Moving a \[size\] \[industry\] from \[old way\] to \[new way\]*
  - Moving a 25-person NVOCC from email threads to a tracked booking pipeline
- *From \[X\] to \[Y\] \[units\] a day: how a \[size\] \[industry\] rebuilt its \[process\]*
  - From 5 to 25 bookings a day: how a 25-person NVOCC rebuilt its booking process
- *How a \[size\] \[industry\] got \[whole team\] working \[process\] in parallel*
  - How a 25-person NVOCC got its whole booking team working in parallel

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *\[Work\] lived in \[inboxes / spreadsheets / one person's head\], so \[no owner / no status / a bottleneck\].*
- Solution: *\[System piece\] that gives every \[item\] a clear owner, status and next step.*

**Results block metrics:** items handled per day or week, turnaround time, team members able to do the work, conversion or error rate.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *A \[size\] \[industry\] with teams in \[locations\] ran every \[process\] through \[old way\]. \[Key items\] all lived in \[place\]. Nobody could see where any single \[item\] stood.*

**Middle sections**

1. **Why \[old way\] breaks down for a \[industry\]:** the industry-specific cause (deadlines, time zones, many parties).
2. **How we split the problem:** the parts of the fix, and why that split.
3. **One section per part:** what was built, described from the user's point of view.
4. **The slowest step, removed:** the single biggest time sink and how it was automated (e.g. live rates via API).
5. **Where it still needs a person:** an honest limit (e.g. lines with no API).

**Central exhibit:** a lifecycle diagram of the new pipeline, stage by stage.

**Closing line formula:** *\[Work\] used to depend on \[one person / one inbox\]. Now \[whole team\] works \[items\] in parallel, handling \[Y\] instead of \[X\].*

**CTA formula:** *Still running \[process\] out of \[old way\]? Email us and we'll map where your \[items\] get stuck today.*

## Framework B: Built to Scale

**Choose when** the win is new or replaced technology: a legacy system migrated, a move to the cloud, or a new website, app, portal or commerce channel. The reader is a founder or tech lead who has seen projects run late or break under growth. Voice: confident, specific, with technical detail kept light.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *Replacing a \[age\]-year-old \[system\] for a \[size\] \[industry\] without a day of downtime*
  - Replacing a 12-year-old ERP for a 60-person distributor without a day of downtime
- *Launching a \[channel\] for a \[size\] \[industry\] in \[timeframe\]*
  - Launching a B2B ordering portal for a 40-person food supplier in 14 weeks
- *How a \[size\] \[industry\] moved \[systems\] to the cloud and \[outcome\]*
  - How a 30-person clinic group moved its patient systems to the cloud and cut IT costs by a third

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *\[Old system / missing channel\] meant \[slow, fragile, costly, or lost customers\].*
- Solution: *\[New platform / channel\] built on \[plain-words description\], with \[migration or launch safeguard\].*

**Results block metrics:** uptime or incidents, page or process speed, users, orders or revenue through the new channel, systems retired, running cost.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *A \[size\] \[industry\] ran \[function\] on \[old system / without a channel\]. It \[specific pain: crashed at peak, couldn't integrate, lost orders\]. They needed \[new capability\] without stopping the business.*

**Middle sections**

1. **What the old setup cost them:** outages, workarounds, lost sales or manual re-entry.
2. **The choices we made and why:** build vs. buy, platform choice, what we kept from the old system.
3. **Moving without stopping:** data migration, parallel running, cut-over plan.
4. **What users see now:** the new experience for staff or customers.
5. **Built to grow:** how it handles more users, locations or products.

**Central exhibit:** a simple before/after architecture sketch, or screenshots of the new channel with client details removed.

**Closing line formula:** *\[Business\] now runs \[function\] on \[new platform\], ready for \[next stage of growth\] without another rebuild.*

**CTA formula:** *Is an old system holding you back? Email us and we'll sketch what replacing it would take, without stopping your business.*

## Framework C: Roadmap and Insight

**Choose when** the main deliverable helped the client decide: a transformation roadmap, a prioritised list of opportunities, or data connected into dashboards and analytics that changed how they make decisions. The reader is a CEO or MD who knows they need to change but not where to start. Voice: clear, strategic, every claim tied to money or time.

**Title formulas (pick one)**

- *Deciding what to digitise first: a transformation roadmap for a \[size\] \[industry\]*
  - Deciding what to digitise first: a transformation roadmap for an 80-person manufacturer
- *How a \[size\] \[industry\] found \[X\] opportunities and chose the \[Y\] worth funding*
  - How a 50-person logistics firm found 14 opportunities and chose the 3 worth funding
- *From \[old reporting\] to \[new insight\]: how a \[size\] \[industry\] now decides \[what\] in \[time\]*
  - From month-end spreadsheets to live dashboards: how a retail group now decides stock in a day

**Table row formula**

- Challenge: *\[Leadership\] knew \[area\] needed to change but \[couldn't see where / had no data / had competing ideas\].*
- Solution: *\[Roadmap / scoring / dashboard\] that ranks \[options\] by \[impact, cost, effort\] and ties each to \[a business outcome\].*

**Results block metrics:** opportunities assessed vs. funded, projected payback or savings, time to produce a report or make a decision, first roadmap item delivered.

**Intro formula (2-3 sentences):** *A \[size\] \[industry\] had \[many ideas / scattered data / a board asking for a digital plan\]. Every department wanted something different, and nobody could compare the options fairly. They asked us to decide what to transform first, and why.*

**Middle sections**

1. **The question we were asked:** in the client's words.
2. **How we assessed it:** interviews, process mapping, data reviewed, and the scoring method.
3. **What we found:** the top opportunities, and one surprising finding.
4. **What we recommended and what we didn't:** the cut list matters as much as the shortlist.
5. **What happened next:** the first item delivered, or the decision it enabled.

**Central exhibit:** the priority roadmap (impact vs. effort) or one anonymised dashboard view.

**Closing line formula:** *\[Client\] now has a funded plan / a live view of \[data\], so \[decision\] takes \[new time\] instead of \[old time or guesswork\].*

**CTA formula:** *Not sure what to transform first? Email us and we'll help you rank your options by what pays back fastest.*

## Results block rules

Show 2 to 4 figures as **Before / Now**, with one small line under them saying how and over what period they were measured. DT buyers think in money and time, so make at least one figure about one of those.

| Metric | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookings handled per day | 5-7 | 20-30 |
| Team members working bookings | 1 | 5 of 5 |
| Time to quote a rate | 1-2 hours of calls | Under 5 minutes |

*Example layout using the NVOCC case. The third row is the kind of figure to add. Measured over \[X\] months of live use.*

**No hard numbers yet? Use one of these instead, in this order of strength:**

1. **A money or time figure with its method:** "About 30 hours a week of calls removed, based on the team's own estimate"
2. **A volume count:** "4,800 bookings processed through the pipeline since go-live"
3. **A retirement fact:** "3 spreadsheets and 2 shared inboxes retired"
4. **A milestone:** "Live for 8 months after a 6-month pilot, with no rollback"

For the NVOCC case, turning "converting more of them into confirmed business" into a number would make it the strongest page on the site.
